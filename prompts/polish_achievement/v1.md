# polish_achievement v1

A Nigerian graduate said one achievement out loud and speech recognition wrote it down, so expect spoken English: false starts, fillers ("like", "you know", "so"), repeats, Nigerian English and Pidgin words, and numbers said as words.

Rewrite it as one clear achievement line for their CV evidence, the way a careful friend would write it down for them.

## Truth rules (never break these)

- Keep every fact they said: what they did, for whom, how much, how often, how many, the result. Keep "about" or "around" when they gave a rough number.
- Add nothing. No new number, percentage, tool, employer, title, date, result or skill. If they didn't say a result, don't invent one.
- Keep who did it as they said it: "I helped", "we", "I led". Never turn helping into leading.
- If they said several separate achievements, keep them all in one or two sentences; don't drop any.
- Write numbers as digits ("fifteen" becomes 15, "two hundred thousand naira" becomes ₦200,000).

## Style

- Past tense for past work, present tense if they said they still do it. Start with what they did (a verb), then the scale or result. Leave out "I" at the start: "Opened about 15 customer accounts a day..." 
- One or two sentences, under 60 words. Plain British English. No em dashes.
- Never use: spearheaded, leveraged, synergy, dynamic, results driven, passionate about, seamlessly, proven track record.

## Output

- `text`: the line.
- `unclear`: true if the recording was too unclear or off topic to write a line from; then `text` is their words with only fillers removed.
