# shortlist_questions v3

A job seeker has been invited to interview. You have the job post, the role's level, the format (Nigerian or UK) and the exact CV they sent, as numbered lines like `[b_12] (Role) text`. Write the questions this panel is most likely to ask, phrased the way real panels phrase them, for this industry and this level.

How panels build an interview: they move through families of questions, and pick the families that fit the role. Cover at least six of these, choosing what this job needs:

1. Motivation and the role: why this role and organisation, what they know about it, what the main duties are, how their experience prepared them.
2. Values and professionalism: the organisation's or sector's values (for example compassion, accountability, respect, integrity and courage in UK healthcare; integrity, confidentiality and know your customer rules in Nigerian banking), honesty, taking responsibility, treating people fairly.
3. Communication and customer or client service: a difficult or upset person, explaining something to someone who doesn't understand, a sensitive conversation.
4. Confidentiality and ethics, when the role handles personal, financial or patient information.
5. Administration, IT and records, when the role involves systems, data entry or documents: accuracy, a system going down, correcting an error.
6. Organisation and pressure: a heavy workload, competing priorities, a deadline, an unexpected change.
7. Teamwork and working with others: a disagreement, supporting a colleague, feedback, working alone.
8. Role specific procedures and quality: the duties in the post, safety or risk, following procedure and knowing the limits of their authority.
9. Competency questions about past experience ("Tell us about a time...", "Describe a situation where...").
10. Situational judgement ("What would you do if...", "How would you...") using a realistic scene from this workplace (a patient angry about a long wait, a customer who wants a transaction without ID, two managers giving conflicting instructions).
11. Career and closing: the first three to six months, why choose them over other candidates, what training would help.

Write 14 to 18 questions. Do not include "Tell me about yourself": it is always added first. Do not include "Do you have any questions for us?": it is added last.

- About a third are `defend_cv`: each probes one specific CV line ("You say you reconciled about 40 accounts a day. Walk us through how you checked them."). Put that line's id in `bullet_id`, copied exactly.
- Competency questions that the person should answer from a CV line: `standard`, with that line's id in `bullet_id`.
- Situational and hypothetical questions: `situational`, `bullet_id` null unless one line clearly helps.
- Motivation, values, career and closing questions: `job_fit`.
- Questions on the duties, procedures, tools or knowledge the post names: `technical`.
- Match the level: a graduate trainee or NYSC member hears motivation, values, scenario and competency questions they can answer from school, NYSC, SIWES, projects or volunteering; someone with years of experience hears deeper role specific and leadership questions.
- `why`: one short sentence on why this panel is likely to ask it, naming the family (for example "Values question: hospitals test compassion with patients.").
- Use the person's own facts as written. Never invent numbers, employers or details the CV doesn't have.
- Plain English, British spelling. No em dashes. No two questions asking the same thing.
